Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 14, 2026

In short: the people, birthdays, dates, notes and settings you enter stay on your phone. There is no account and no sign-up, and your contacts are read on the device and never uploaded.

  • A few services help run the app: Apple (App Store and payments) or Google (Google Play and payments), depending on your phone, RevenueCat (checks whether Pro is active and, while the sharing switch is on, receives a few rounded usage counts), Sentry (receives crash and error reports while the same switch is on) and Cloudflare (serves our website, the list of gift partners and the forwarding address for partner links).
  • Usage counts and crash reports are sent only while the switch “Share usage data and crash reports” is on (Settings › About). In the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it is off until you agree. Elsewhere it is on until you switch it off.
  • We do not sell this data, do not use it to build advertising profiles and do not combine it with data from other companies. The app does not use the advertising identifier.
  • Links to gift partners open in your browser (Safari on iPhone). Some of them are affiliate links: we may earn a commission if you buy.

This policy covers the iPhone app and the Android app (both called “the app” below) and the website didyou.alexanderburkhard.com. We have not appointed a data protection officer, because the law does not require one for us.

3. What stays on your device

The app stores the following only on your device, in its own storage:

  • The people you add or import: name, birthday and other dates (such as anniversaries), a phone number if you add one, and a link to their contact card.
  • Your notes (for example gift ideas) and your choices per person (reminder style, gift reminders, preferred way to send wishes).
  • When you marked a birthday as done, your settings, and your answer to the question “Get gift deals before birthdays and holidays?”.
  • Counters the app keeps for the usage counts in section 9 and for deciding when to ask whether you like the app (numbers and days only). Your answer to that question stays on your device. If you answer yes, on iPhone Apple’s own rating dialog may follow; Apple runs it. On Android the app opens its page in Google Play, where you can write a review.
  • A copy of the gift partner list, and of its pictures if it has any (section 11).

We cannot see any of this. On iPhone, like other app data, it can be part of your iPhone or iCloud backups if you use them; those backups are governed by Apple’s privacy policy. On Android, Google’s automatic backup of app data covers only one tiny file, the early-bird record (section 8); your people, notes and settings are not part of it. That backup is governed by Google’s privacy policy.

4. Contacts

With your permission, the app reads names, birthdays, other dates, phone numbers and contact photos from your Contacts to find birthdays and show them. If you choose to save a birthday to a contact, the app writes only the birthday or date field of that contact, or creates a new contact with the name and birthday you entered. It never deletes contacts and never changes any other field. On iPhone you can share all or only selected contacts and change this at any time in iOS Settings (Apps › this app › Contacts). On Android the app asks once for permission to read and to write contacts; you can take it back at any time in Android Settings (Apps › this app › Permissions). Nothing from your Contacts is sent to us, to RevenueCat, to Sentry or to anyone else.

5. Notifications and widgets

Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device. There is no push service and no server of ours involved. A notification can contain a person’s name, because you entered it, and it can appear on your lock screen.

On Android the app asks for permission to show notifications, and offers to switch on “Alarms & reminders” so a reminder can arrive on the exact minute. Both are used only to deliver reminders. The app also restarts its reminders after the phone has been switched off and on, and can ask Android to let it keep running in the background. These are used only to deliver reminders; nothing about your battery is sent to anyone.

Gift reminders are on by default for every person: a local reminder some days before a birthday (a week, unless you change it) that opens the gift page. You can switch them off per person.

Widgets read the app’s data on your device (on iPhone a small file the app keeps, on Android the app’s own storage).

6. Sending wishes

When you choose to send a birthday message, the app hands the text, and for WhatsApp (and, on Android, for SMS) the person’s phone number, to Messages, your SMS app, WhatsApp or another app you pick. What happens next is between you and that app. The app does not see or store what you send.

7. Purchases and Pro (Apple, Google, RevenueCat)

Pro is sold through Apple’s App Store on iPhone and through Google Play on Android. Apple or Google handles the payment and your Apple Account or Google Account; we never see your payment details.

To find out whether Pro is active and to show current prices, the app uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., USA), which works for us as a processor and stores the data on servers in the USA. The app contacts RevenueCat whenever it starts, whether or not you ever buy anything, and RevenueCat receives:

  • a random app user ID, made on first launch. It is not derived from your name, your Apple Account or your contacts;
  • on iPhone, the identifier for vendor (IDFV), an ID that Apple assigns to all apps from the same developer on your device. On Android there is no such device identifier; the random app user ID above is all that identifies the app;
  • your purchase history for this app (purchases, renewals, cancellations, refunds);
  • technical details: app version, system version, device model, up to three preferred languages, the country of your App Store or Google Play account and whether the build is a test build;
  • your IP address, which every internet connection necessarily reveals.

RevenueCat sends back the settings we manage there: the number of people that are free, which plan is shown first, and the end date of the early-bird offer (section 8).

RevenueCat does not receive your contacts, birthdays, notes or name. This part works independently of the sharing switch, because the app cannot show prices or unlock Pro without it. Legal basis: performing the purchase contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) for people who buy, and our legitimate interest in running the paywall and the free limit (Art. 6(1)(f)) for the check at launch. To the extent the law requires consent for reading or storing information on your device, this is covered by the exception for what is strictly necessary to provide a service you asked for (section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

See RevenueCat’s privacy policy, Apple’s privacy policy and Google’s privacy policy.

8. Early bird

Everyone who first got the app before a cutoff date gets Pro for free, for good. To check this on iPhone, the app asks Apple (through StoreKit) for the signed date on which you first got the app. On Android, the app takes the earlier of the date Android recorded for the install and the date RevenueCat first saw the app on your phone (RevenueCat already holds that date for the random app user ID). The comparison with the cutoff and the result happen on your phone and are stored there; on Android that small file is also part of Google’s automatic backup (section 3) so that Pro for early birds survives a new phone. The date from Apple or Android is not sent to us or to RevenueCat.

9. Usage counts (the switch “Share usage data and crash reports”)

With the switch on, the app attaches a few facts about how it is used to the same random app user ID at RevenueCat. They are categories, days and rounded ranges. Names, phone numbers, birthdays, dates, notes and any other content from your contacts are never sent: the app can only send values from a fixed list, so free text cannot be sent. This is the complete list for this version:

  • Where the Pro offer was opened from (first and last time), how often you saw it (1, 2–3, 4–9 or 10 and more), whether you reached the free limit, and the day that first happened.
  • How many birthdays were found in your Contacts and how many people are active in the app, as ranges (0, 1–10, 11–25, 26–50, 51–100, more than 100), and how many other dates, such as anniversaries, you use (0, 1–3, 4 or more).
  • The reminder level you chose, whether gift reminders are on, your answer to the gift deals question (yes, no, not asked), and whether a widget is installed.
  • Whether notifications are allowed, denied, provisional (iPhone only) or not yet asked, and whether access to Contacts is full, limited (iPhone only) or none. On Android also whether the phone lets the app set alarms on the exact minute (allowed or denied).
  • How often you tapped Done on the day and later (rounded), and how many reminders it took (rounded).
  • How far you got in the setup, and the language the app is shown in.
  • Whether your Pro comes from the early-bird offer (yes or no).

How the switch works:

  • EU, EEA, UK, Switzerland: the switch is off until you answer the question on the last setup screen (“Share” or “Don’t share”, equal weight). Nothing is sent before you agree. The app decides where you are from the country of your App Store or Google Play account; only if that country is unknown does it use the country of your SIM card or mobile network (Android) or the region set on your phone.
  • Everywhere else: the switch is on, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings › About. Because the app goes by the country of your App Store or Google Play account, the switch also starts on if that account belongs to a country outside the regions above, wherever you are.
  • While the switch is off, the app still counts on your device. If you turn it on later, the counts so far are sent once.
  • Turning it off stops sending, for the usage counts and for crash reports. What was sent earlier is deleted on request, using your Support ID (section 17).
  • One switch covers both purposes, usage counts and crash reports. You cannot choose one without the other.

Legal basis: in the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; for reading and storing information on your device, section 25(1) TDDDG). Elsewhere our legitimate interest in understanding and improving the app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), with the right to object by turning the switch off.

10. Crash and error reports (Sentry)

While the same switch is on, the app sends crash reports and a small number of error reports to Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., doing business as Sentry, USA), which works for us as a processor. Our Sentry project stores its data in Sentry’s EU region, in Frankfurt, Germany. The Android app reports to its own project in the same Sentry organisation, also stored in Sentry’s EU region in Frankfurt, under the same rules: only while the same switch is on, crashes and the error reports listed here, no user ID or device ID, no screenshots, screen contents, performance traces, session recordings or logs, and the same removal of names, phone numbers and e-mail addresses before a report leaves the phone. The error reports cover: a reminder could not be scheduled or shown, a write to Contacts failed, a purchase or restore failed, a backup file could not be imported. Each report names only the place in the app and the system’s error type and number, never a message and never the contact concerned.

A report contains: the error, a technical stack trace, the app version and build, the device model, the system version, the language and time zone settings, some device state (on iPhone for example free memory, low power mode and temperature state; on Android the screen size) and a short trail of app events such as “went to the background”.

A report does not contain: a user ID, an ID of your device (the app removes the identifiers that Sentry’s software would add), your name or device name, an e-mail address, screenshots, screen contents, performance traces, session recordings or logs. Before a report leaves the phone, the app removes user and request data and replaces anything that looks like the name of one of your people, a phone number or an e-mail address.

The app sends no IP address, but the connection itself reveals one to Sentry. Our project is set not to store IP addresses. Sentry may still derive an approximate location (such as country and city) from the connection and attach it to the report.

Legal basis: as in section 9. Sentry deletes reports automatically after at most 90 days.

11. Gift ideas, partner links and deals

The gift partner list. The gift page shows partners (for example flowers, gift cards, experiences). The app downloads the list of partners, and any pictures it refers to, from didyou.alexanderburkhard.com, at most once a day, as a plain request without cookies and without identifiers. Once the app holds a copy, the request asks only whether the list has changed since that copy. The server therefore sees your IP address, the time and the technical request data, as every server does, and a generic app name (“DidYou”) as the user agent. The list is the same for everyone; which partners are shown is decided on your phone from the country of your App Store or Google Play account. Pictures, if there are any, come only from our own website, and the app asks only for those it needs for your country. The website is served by Cloudflare, which processes this data to deliver the files. We do not store it. When you open the gift page, nothing is loaded from a partner’s server, so no partner learns anything from it. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (running the gift page); section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG.

Partner links. Tapping a partner opens its website in your browser (Safari on iPhone), never in a view inside the app. The link goes through a forwarding address on our website (didyou.alexanderburkhard.com/go/…), which sends you on to the partner and adds one to a daily total for the combination of day, partner, country (two letters, taken from the country of your App Store or Google Play account) and placement in the app (for example gift page or gift reminder). It does not store your IP address, user agent, cookies, referrer, identifiers or the link itself, and the totals cannot be traced to a person. They are kept in a Cloudflare database in Western Europe. The request reaches our website like any other (section 12). The forwarding step passes no referrer to the partner. The address the partner receives contains no name, date, contact or device information: only the partner’s own address, our affiliate ID and one fixed label for the placement.

Partners are third parties. After the forwarding step, the partner’s website and its affiliate network process your visit under their own privacy policies; they can set cookies to credit a purchase. Some links are affiliate links: if you buy through one, we may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. The gift page says so. Tapping a link unlocks nothing and rewards nothing.

Gift deals (opt-in). In setup the app asks “Get gift deals before birthdays and holidays?”, with “Yes, send me deals” and “No thanks”. With yes, gift reminders may mention discounts, and the app sends holiday gift reminders (for example Valentine’s Day, Mother’s Day, Father’s Day, the Christmas order deadline). With no, or with no answer yet, gift reminders use neutral wording and there are no holiday reminders. Yes is your consent to these notifications, including where they count as advertising (section 7 UWG). The answer is a setting on your device that you can change at any time under Settings › Gift reminders. No notification ever contains a partner name, a link or a deal code. Deals shown on the gift page are visible to everyone. The answer is not sent to partners. If the switch in section 9 is on, only the coarse value (yes, no, not asked) is part of the usage counts.

12. Website

The website sets no cookies and loads no scripts, fonts or images from third parties. We do not look at individual visits; Cloudflare may show us aggregated traffic figures such as the number of requests. Cloudflare serves the website and processes your IP address and request data to deliver pages and protect its network (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). It also adds a technical header that lets some browsers (not Safari) send Cloudflare a short report if a page fails to load. We do not store this data. If you write to us by e-mail, we process your address and message to answer you (Art. 6(1)(b) or (f) GDPR).

13. Backups you export

If you use Export backup, the app creates a file with your people, dates, notes and settings and lets you save or share it where you choose. That file is yours. Keep it somewhere private, because anyone with the file can read it. The deals answer is not part of it.

16. How long we keep data

  • On your device: until you delete it in the app or delete the app.
  • RevenueCat: until we delete the record or close our RevenueCat account. We delete a record on request.
  • Sentry: reports are deleted automatically after at most 90 days.
  • Gift partner list requests and website visits: we store nothing ourselves. Cloudflare keeps technical logs for a limited time under its own terms.
  • Click totals: daily totals without personal data, kept without a fixed end.
  • E-mails to us: until the matter is closed, then deleted within 12 months unless a legal duty to keep them applies.

17. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and, for processing based on legitimate interest, objection (Art. 15–21). If you gave consent, you can withdraw it at any time with effect for the future, by turning the switch off in Settings › About. Withdrawal does not affect what happened before. You can change your gift deals answer at any time under Settings › Gift reminders.

Because your own data is stored only on your device, you are in full control of it: edit or remove people in the app, and delete the app to erase its data from your phone. On Android, the early-bird record in Google’s backup can be removed in your Google Account’s backup settings.

For data held by RevenueCat there is no account to look you up by, so we need your Support ID. You find it in Settings › About; tap it to copy, and include it in your e-mail to ab@alexanderburkhard.com. With it we can find your record, send you a copy and delete it. We answer within one month.

Crash and error reports carry no user ID and no device ID, so we cannot tell which reports belong to you. Under Art. 11 GDPR the rights of access, rectification, erasure and portability therefore do not apply to them unless you give us information that lets us identify a report. They are deleted automatically after the period in section 16.

In the UK this is the Information Commissioner’s Office, in Switzerland the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.

19. Children

The app is not directed at children under 16, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you think a child has sent us personal data, write to us and we will delete it.

20. Other regions

Data protection law differs around the world. The rights in section 17 are offered to everyone who asks, wherever they live. California: we do not sell personal information and do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, as California law defines these terms. Quebec: the person in charge of the protection of personal information is Alexander Burkhard (contact details in section 1).

21. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new date. If a change affects what the app sends and your consent is needed, the app asks again.
